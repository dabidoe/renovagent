1. We go to "example project", not example projects.
The Homes & projects modal offers one **Explore the example project** button (`apps/web/src/app/workspace-app.tsx`), which opens the single Hutchinson house. The home page's whole studio section is also just the Hutchinson living room (`studio-preview.tsx` renders `HUTCHINSON.rooms[0]`). The gallery with all four examples plus the + card exists at `#examples`, but the entry points funnel to the one house. (A local change already re-points the home CTA at the gallery; production still has the old button until it ships.)

2. No example or studio view has a good layout for furniture placement.
Placing and arranging furniture (the 3D layout / placement controls) feels buried and cramped everywhere: examples, own studio, directions. Paint and floors own the panel; furniture is a side quest. For a renovation product this is backwards — the seating/bed/table plan is the design.

3. Paint is six swatches with no clear "pick new".
The studio shows six Sherwin-Williams presets (`PRESET_COLORS` in `paint-floor.ts`). A fuller brand/family/search picker exists, but it hides behind an **All colours** toggle and needs the connected paint catalog — on a plain local demo it has nothing to query, so for most first looks the six dots are the whole story. No custom hex/eyedropper entry anywhere.

4. Flooring is five lookalikes, and the hardwood renders look cheap.
Five oak styles (light, white, honey, dark walnut, gray-washed in `packages/core/src/commerce/floor-styles.ts`) times LVP/hardwood, and the differences read as filters rather than materials — the hardwood previews don't look like wood you'd pay for. Meanwhile the Budget / Mid-range / Premium tier chips plus paint-line plus sheen chips stack up into selector clutter that eats the panel without helping anyone decide.

5. Home should present the examples, not just Hutchinson.
Same root as (1), from the top of the funnel: a first-time visitor meets one Philadelphia room and could easily conclude that's the product. The Oakland, Brooklyn, and bedroom-refresh examples (and the + Your project card) should be one click from the hero, not hiding behind it.

6. In the bedroom, picked furniture shows as swatches, not things to buy.
`inspo-example.tsx` lists each selected item as a colour dot plus a name, with bare store-name links underneath and no "Search:" qualifier (the inspiration panel and room plan both label theirs now). No thumbnails, no product pages, no sense of what the rust pillow or green lamp actually are. A picked item should read as a row: preview image, name, price-or-unpriced state, link.

"You know, THIS would be nice" — from those breaks
* One rule for entries: every "examples" affordance opens the gallery; the single house is a card inside it, never the destination. Rename the modal button to match.
* A placement-first studio layout: furniture/layout as a first-class tab with room to breathe, finishes as the second act — or at minimum a layout that doesn't bury placement below the fold.
* A real paint control: keep the six presets for speed, add an always-visible custom colour entry (hex + common-brand search that works from bundled data), and say what "All colours" needs when the catalog isn't connected.
* Fewer, more honest floor options: cut near-duplicate oaks or differentiate them, fix the hardwood renders (grain, plank direction, light response), and collapse tier/line/sheen into one compact "materials & budget" row.
* Furniture rows everywhere an item is picked: thumbnail (cropped outline or generated still), name, allowance-or-unpriced, and a labeled search/product link — the same row in the bedroom example, the room plan, and the shopping list.
