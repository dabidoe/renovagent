# Renovagent after the hackathon

**Decision draft · November 10, 2026**

Build **visual preconstruction and purchasing coordination for small residential remodelers**. After the site visit, Renovagent gives the homeowner and remodeler one version of what will change, what products are planned, who supplies them, and what the contractor needs to price or verify. The first buyer is the owner or estimator at a small design-build or kitchen-and-bath firm. The homeowner can review the plan and choose products for free. The remodeler pays for the work of turning those choices into a proposal and a reviewed purchase package.

The October plan began with homeowner purchasing. The first post-hackathon draft moved the sale upstream and deferred procurement. This revision brings **product selection and purchase preparation into the first paid workflow**, where a product choice can change labor, allowances, and lead time. A single bathroom is the first playbook. The property and project model stays general, and the core workflow runs without a sponsor, a Meta account, a model API, or special capture hardware. [October product scope](RENOVAGENT_PRODUCT_SCOPE.md) · [October technical plan](RENOVAGENT_TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md)

## Where we have to win

Handoff already converts phone walkthroughs into notes and estimates and documents a beta room scan. Houzz Pro already generates an editable estimate or proposal from a 3D floor plan. magicplan already exports plans, geometry, and estimate breakdowns. The workflow advantage to test is whether **the remodeler can reconcile a homeowner’s product choice with the install scope, price, and purchasing responsibility in one revision**, with less re-entry than their current process. We should test that against the firm’s actual tools and last comparable job. [Handoff walkthrough](https://help.handoff.ai/en/articles/13251817-how-to-capture-a-walkthrough-on-your-phone) · [Handoff room scan](https://help.handoff.ai/en/articles/15984219-how-to-create-your-first-project-and-estimate) · [Houzz Pro floor plan to proposal](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/how-to-create-an-estimate-using-your-3d-floor-plan) · [magicplan exports](https://help.magicplan.app/export-formats)

The customer report points to unpaid proposal work, hard-to-compare scopes, and revisions spread across drawings, spreadsheets, and texts. Those are leads for interviews. Houzz’s survey found that 37% of respondents exceeded their renovation budget in 2025 and that scope or design changes were a factor for some. Neither a survey nor a contest result establishes how often a remodeler would use Renovagent or what they would pay. [Customer research supplied with this draft](<../deep-research-report customers.md>) · [Houzz study](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/8-home-remodeling-trends-to-watch-in-2026-stsetivw-vs~184900562)

## The first thing to sell

**Pilot offer.** For **$199 per bathroom**, use contractor-supplied photos, measurements, notes, and one homeowner product choice to prepare a visual scope, editable budget worksheet, and contractor-reviewed purchase list. Include one revision and aim for an initial draft in two business days after receiving usable inputs. Final review timing depends on the contractor. Start with two manually fulfilled design-partner jobs, then quote the same offer to the next qualified firms and invoice manually. This is a price test for a hands-on service.

The package has four parts, all labeled with the same revision:

1. A before-and-after plan or simple 3D view with a trade-grouped scope, supporting photos or measurements, exclusions, and open site questions.
2. A product schedule with exact manufacturer model and merchant SKU where confirmed, relevant specs and evidence, quantities, and a supply status for each line. Use **homeowner-supplied**, **contractor-supplied**, **allowance**, or **TBD**. Record contractor-provided conditions for assembly, delivery, warranty responsibility, and substitutions where relevant.
3. A worksheet with supported quantities, contractor-supplied labor rates and allowances, dated price observations or quotes, and any contractor-confirmed schedule impact. Missing inputs stay visible. Keep the contractor’s account pricing private unless they choose to share it. Distinguish price observations, planning allowances, and the final merchant total.
4. A client PDF, estimator CSV, and approved-item purchase list with merchant links or a supported cart link. Show the buyer, merchant, quantities, and what to verify at checkout. Hold unresolved items out of the purchase list and show them separately. For the pilot, the designated buyer checks out with the merchant and may use their own trade account.

**First job.** Keep the existing bathroom shell and openings. The homeowner selects a different vanity after seeing the plan. Record the candidate SKU and manufacturer specs, compare the footprint and stated connections to recorded site measurements, and ask the contractor about assembly and drain fit. If the contractor confirms added assembly labor, show that in the revised scope and worksheet. If a concealed connection needs verification, keep the selection pending. A required service relocation is outside this playbook. When the contractor clears a supported option and the homeowner accepts the revised plan, add that exact item to the purchase list. Keep the prior issued version available. Planning agreement, product review, spend authorization, and construction release are separate decisions.

## Bring procurement forward in stages

1. **Prepare and hand off now.** Reuse the existing catalog, compatibility checks, purchase plan, and checkout review. The pilot can use manufacturer specs, contractor quotes, dated observations, and merchant links. Existing demo catalog data remains labeled as example data. Current checkout review prepares Amazon cart links for supported ASIN groups and otherwise hands off product links. Live stock, final checkout totals, supplier account connections, and order placement are separate work. [Commerce catalog](architecture/commerce-catalog.md) · [Checkout handoff](architecture/agent-procurement-checkout.md) · [Retail ingestion baseline](architecture/retail-pricing-access.md)
2. **Prove one supplier connection.** Let completed pilot purchases and the buyer’s actual supplier determine the first integration. Start with authorized price/stock lookup, a quote, or cart transfer. Lowe’s now documents catalog, orders and UCP checkout paths. Ferguson documents PunchOut for purchasing systems and publishes API terms. Home Depot documents a Pro Xtra partnership with Buildertrend, whose automatic Home Depot catalog sync was retired in July 2026 while receipt integration continued. These are candidates and precedents. Record eligibility, data rights, credentials, trade-price treatment, and sandbox results before scheduling integration work. [Lowe’s Developer Hub](https://developer.lowes.com/portal/solutions/) · [Ferguson PunchOut](https://www.ferguson.com/sites/content/docs/2600007495006) · [Ferguson API terms](https://developer.ferguson.com/sites/default/files/2024-08/Ferguson%20Developer%20Terms%20of%20Use.pdf) · [Home Depot Pro integrations](https://www.homedepot.com/c/pro-integrations) · [Buildertrend Marketplace FAQ](https://buildertrend.com/help-article/buildertrend-marketplace-faqs/)
3. **Execute only after supervised validation.** Before any order, show the authorized buyer the exact revision, merchant, items and quantities, all-in total, destination and delivery option. Record a separate, expiring approval bound to those details and recheck them before submission. Changes require renewed approval. Make retries safe, reconcile ambiguous responses with the merchant before retrying, and store order confirmation against the approved lines. Keep human checkout available. UCP or ACP may provide the interface when the selected partner supports it. Merchant access and authorization still have to be established. [Google UCP guide](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp) · [OpenAI Agentic Checkout specification](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/checkout)
4. **Coordinate what happens next.** Once there are real orders to reconcile, test receipts and delivery exceptions. Feed a backorder or proposed substitution into a new review of scope, budget, and schedule. Use the same revision history for later change-order and milestone requests. Preserve actual work and order history, and leave external commitments to authorized users.

## 90 days to a decision

| Window | Work | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Days 1–30** | Contact 20 qualified remodelers in one reachable market and interview five about a recent proposal revision and an owner-selected item. Deliver two concierge design-partner packages, then quote the paid pilot. Measure active contractor time, actual client sends, unpriced lines, and product review or handoff usage. | Aim for **three paying firms** and at least **three comparable revisions** with a median **30% reduction in contractor hands-on time** against a recorded baseline, with no unflagged material scope omissions in contractor review. If volume falls short, keep the evidence and extend the manual test before funding automation. |
| **Days 31–60** | If the first gate is met, automate the repeated proposal-to-purchase-list steps. Track which approved lists reach checkout and verify use with an order confirmation or contractor confirmation where permission allows. Measure wrong SKU/quantity, blocked items, and rework. | Look for **two firms placing a second paid project within 60 days of their first**, and **two real purchases using the reviewed list** before allocating an integration build. One named supplier must recur and have a viable authorized access path. |
| **Days 61–90** | If the procurement gate is met, try one supplier connection in a sandbox. Start with lookup, quote, or cart transfer. Separately test the Project Agent price when delivery cost supports it, and offer Pro to repeat buyers with enough live work to judge it. | Compare paid repeat, purchase completion, exceptions and direct contribution. Any later supervised live ordering needs explicit buyer authorization and a tested recovery path. If the supplier gate fails, keep link-based checkout and diagnose the friction before investing in an adapter. |

Track active contractor minutes separately from elapsed turnaround. Track founder outreach time for acquisition, plus intake, research, QA, support, revision labor, compute, and payment fees per job. Direct contribution is collected revenue less those direct fulfillment costs, including human time at a consistent internal rate. For a **$99** pass, a proposed **75% direct contribution margin** leaves **$24.75** per project for all direct costs. An **$8** compute budget leaves **$16.75** for fees and labor. Measure the actual costs before offering the price at scale. These prices and gates are pilot hypotheses.

If firms pay for product reconciliation and use a simple plan, simplify the 3D. If they repeatedly send the scope and ignore the purchase list, keep the paid proposal workflow and interview them about where purchasing already works. If they buy through the list and consistently return to one supplier, make that handoff better before expanding into delivery, change orders, invoicing, or punch lists.
